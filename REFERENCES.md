# PM Challenge — Reference Library

Seven verified voices. Each entry: core principle → one-line distillation → source URLs → when to invoke.

## Cat Wu — Head of Product, Claude Code and Cowork at Anthropic

**Core principle:** Speed is the strategy. Ship in research preview by default, remove scaffolding as underlying capabilities improve, and treat engineering and product as a single function — not handoffs.

**Distillation:** Build before the model is ready. Velocity compounds. UI crutches that paper over model gaps slow you down when the model catches up.

**Sources:**
- [Product management on the AI exponential](https://claude.com/blog/product-management-on-the-ai-exponential) — Claude.ai blog, Cat's own writing
- [How Anthropic's product team moves faster than anyone else](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-anthropics-product-team-moves) — Lenny's Newsletter interview

**Invoke when:**
- Someone is over-designing before shipping to learn
- A brief is optimizing for polish over learning
- Scope is bloated because the team is afraid to ship early
- The plan assumes a waterfall handoff between strategy and execution

## Claire Vo — CPO at LaunchDarkly, founder of ChatPRD

**Core principle:** The AI-native PM doesn't write about building — they build. Spec-driven development, rapid prototyping with AI, and treating ChatPRD like a CPO you can interrogate in real time.

**Distillation:** Stop writing briefs that describe what engineering should build. Start using AI to prototype, test, and validate before a single sprint is planned. Product management is dead as a gatekeeping role — it survives as a builder role.

**Sources:**
- [ChatPRD blog](https://www.chatprd.ai/blog) — Claire's primary writing on AI-native PM workflows
- [Product Management Is Dead](https://www.chatprd.ai/blog/product-management-is-dead) — the defining framing piece
- [How I AI podcast](https://every.to/podcast/best-of-the-pod-she-built-an-ai-product-manager-bringing-in-six-figures-as-a-side-hustle) — practical AI workflow examples

**Invoke when:**
- A brief could be replaced by a working prototype
- The plan involves weeks of discovery that could be compressed with AI-assisted research
- The PM role is being used as a translation layer between users and engineers, not a value-add
- Someone is writing docs instead of validating assumptions

## Shreyas Doshi — Former PM leader at Stripe, Twitter, Google, Yahoo

**Core principle:** LNO (Leverage / Neutral / Overhead) — not all tasks return equal value, and treating them equally is the root of PM burnout and underperformance. High-agency PMs shape the work; low-agency PMs execute what's handed to them.

**Distillation:** Ask: is this task Leverage (10x return), Neutral (1:1 return), or Overhead (necessary cost)? PMs who conflate these work hard and produce little. High-agency PMs pick leverage and deprioritize the rest without waiting for permission.

**Sources:**
- [LNO Framework (Coda)](https://coda.io/@shreyas/lno-framework) — the original write-up
- [Shreyas on Lenny's Podcast](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/episode-3-shreyas-doshi) — LNO, pre-mortems, three levels of product work, strategy vs. execution problems

**Invoke when:**
- A plan looks busy but it's unclear what's actually high-leverage
- Trade-offs aren't stated — the work reads like everything is equally important
- Someone is waiting for permission to prioritize instead of making the call
- The brief is heavy on Overhead and Neutral tasks dressed up as strategy

## Marty Cagan — Founder, Silicon Valley Product Group (SVPG)

**Core principle:** Empowered product teams are assigned outcomes to achieve, not features to build. Good product teams celebrate business impact. Bad teams celebrate shipping. Product discovery is a continuous process, not a phase.

**Distillation:** If your brief is a feature list, you're a feature team. If it's an outcome with a problem to solve, you're a product team. The difference isn't semantic — it's whether engineering is a factory or a collaborator.

**Sources:**
- [Empowered Product Teams](https://www.svpg.com/empowered-product-teams/) — SVPG blog
- [Product vs. Feature Teams](https://www.svpg.com/product-vs-feature-teams/) — SVPG blog
- [The Product Operating Model: An Introduction](https://www.svpg.com/the-product-operating-model-an-introduction/) — SVPG blog

**Invoke when:**
- Success is defined as "launch X feature by [date]" rather than a behavior change
- Engineering is being treated as an execution resource, not a discovery partner
- The brief describes output (what will ship) rather than outcomes (what will change)
- There's no product discovery planned before committing to scope

## Teresa Torres — Product discovery coach, author of *Continuous Discovery Habits*

**Core principle:** Product teams should be in weekly contact with customers — not as a quarterly research exercise, but as an ongoing operating rhythm. The Opportunity Solution Tree maps desired outcomes → opportunities → solutions → experiments. Every plan should have an explicit assumption map and a cheap test before committing to build.

**Distillation:** Discovery isn't a phase before delivery. It's a habit. The question is always: what's the cheapest experiment that teaches us something we don't know?

**Sources:**
- [Continuous Discovery Habits](https://www.amazon.com/Continuous-Discovery-Habits-Discover-Products/dp/1736633309) — the book (2021)
- [Product Talk blog](https://www.producttalk.org) — Teresa's primary writing

**Invoke when:**
- The "test" in the plan is really the full launch
- Research is described as something that happened in the past to justify the plan, not something ongoing
- Assumptions aren't mapped or ranked by risk
- No small experiment is planned before committing to a full build
- The team is deciding between solutions without an explicit opportunity definition

## Julie Zhuo — Former VP Product Design at Facebook, author of *The Making of a Manager*

**Core principle:** Evaluate decisions by the quality of the reasoning, not the outcome. A good decision made with bad reasoning is just luck. Separate Looks / Feels / Works as distinct tests — a thing can look finished, feel right, and still not work.

**Distillation:** Are you evaluating your decisions or your outcomes? They're not the same thing. A plan that "worked" despite bad reasoning is a liability — you won't know why it worked, and you'll repeat the bad reasoning.

**Sources:**
- [The Making of a Manager](https://www.juliezhuo.com/book/manager.html) — the book
- [Julie Zhuo's site](https://www.juliezhuo.com/) — essays and writing

**Invoke when:**
- Someone is justifying a decision by pointing to a past win ("this worked before")
- The plan confuses polish (Looks) with soundness (Works)
- Success criteria mix subjective feel with measurable outcomes
- Post-mortems or retrospectives aren't planned — there's no mechanism to learn from outcomes

## Lenny Rachitsky — Writer, advisor, former Head of Supply Growth at Airbnb

**Core principle:** Great PMs use explicit prioritization frameworks, benchmark against what actually works at comparable companies, and treat PM skills as measurable, not intuitive.

**Distillation:** Stop prioritizing by gut. Use a framework — RICE, ICE, opportunity score — and be explicit about the inputs. The difference between good and great PMs is legible reasoning, not better instincts.

**Sources:**
- [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/) — primary writing on PM careers, prioritization, growth
- [Shreyas Doshi episode](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/episode-3-shreyas-doshi) — LNO + strategy vs. execution problems
- [Cat Wu episode](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-anthropics-product-team-moves) — AI product velocity

**Invoke when:**
- Prioritization is stated but the reasoning is implicit
- A recommendation lacks comparative context ("here's what works at companies like ours")
- The brief doesn't distinguish between high-confidence and low-confidence bets
- Career-level PM skill development is part of the conversation

## Elena Verna — Head of Growth at Lovable; former growth leader at Dropbox, SurveyMonkey, Miro, Amplitude

**Core principle:** Growth isn't a bolt-on department — it's a set of loops (acquisition, activation, engagement, monetization, referral) that the product itself should drive. Nail product-led retention (activation + engagement) before chasing acquisition or monetization; funnels create silos, loops force cross-functional ownership.

**Distillation:** If retention doesn't work, no amount of acquisition or monetization work saves the plan. Growth is the connective tissue between product, marketing, and sales — not a separate team's problem.

**Sources:**
- [Elena's Growth Scoop](https://www.elenaverna.com) — primary writing on PLG, monetization, growth leadership
- [To PLG or not PLG?](https://www.elenaverna.com/p/to-plg-or-not-plg) — the core framing piece on growth loops vs. funnels

**Invoke when:**
- A plan chases acquisition or paid growth before retention/activation is solid
- Growth is treated as a bolt-on team or channel rather than a cross-functional loop
- The brief separates "product" and "growth" work as if they don't feed each other
- Monetization strategy is disconnected from the value the product actually delivers
